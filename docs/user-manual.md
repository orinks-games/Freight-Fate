# Freight Fate Player Manual

Welcome to Freight Fate. In this game you haul freight across American highways and build a trucking career from the ground up. Every run is more than holding the throttle: you plan your fuel, watch your legal hours, look after your truck, and bring the load in safely and on time.

If you are brand new, you do not have to juggle everything on day one. Driving assistance can steer, hold lanes, and brake for you while you get comfortable with the road sounds and controls, and you can dial that help back whenever you want more of the wheel yourself.

Start at the [main menu](#main-menu), then walk through [your first delivery](#quick-start). Whenever you need a shortcut, [driving controls](#driving-controls) lists every key and controller button. Inside the game, **How to play** gives you a shorter guide that always names the keys you have bound.

## Main menu

The first time you launch Freight Fate, it asks how much driving assistance you want, then drops you onto the main menu. From here you can jump straight into **New career**, or poke around the help, sounds, settings, and online screens first.

### New career

Pick **New career** to create a driver. You will type a name, decide whether to start as a company driver or an owner-operator, and choose a home terminal. The [quick start](#quick-start) walks you through those choices and gets you through your first load.

### Achievements

Before you have a saved career, **Achievements** simply tells you there are no saved careers yet. Once you have started playing, pick any saved driver here to browse what they have earned and what is still locked.

### Online

**Online** is where you browse public driver lists, check account-wide achievements, connect your orinks.net account, manage cloud backups and restores, and toggle Discord presence. Each feature is independent, so you can use one without turning on the rest.

### How to play

**How to play** opens the built-in guide. You can listen to a whole page straight through or step through it a chunk at a time. See [How to play controls](#how-to-play-controls) for the reading keys.

### Learn game sounds

Not sure what a warning chime or road cue means? Open **Learn game sounds** from the main menu or the pause menu, pick a sound to hear it in isolation, and read what it is telling you before you run into it at highway speed.

### Practice keys and buttons

**Practice keys and buttons** lets you test any key, controller button, trigger, or stick without sending it to the truck. Press something and the game tells you what it does. For example, "E: Engine on or off" or "Right bumper plus X: Posted speed limit." It always reflects your own bindings from Settings, Gameplay, Controls.

You can open practice from any screen, even in the middle of a drive, with **Shift+F1**, or by holding **Back** and pressing **Start** on a controller. The drive pauses while you practice. Press **Escape** twice, or **Start** twice, to leave.

### Settings

**Settings** is organized into Gameplay, Audio, Speech, Updates, and Problem reports. Driving assistance lives under Gameplay, and individual sound volumes live under Audio.

### Report a problem

If something goes wrong, **Report a problem** opens the report form on orinks.net in your browser with your game log already attached. Tell us what happened, add your email if you want a reply, and submit. If orinks.net is unreachable, it opens the GitHub bug-report page instead and tells you where to find the log so you can attach it yourself.

If the game closed unexpectedly on your last run, the main menu asks once whether you want to report it. Choosing **Report it** opens the same form with that previous run's log attached.

### Quit

**Quit** closes the game.

### Once you have a career

As soon as you have at least one saved driver, three extra choices show up at the top of the main menu:

- **Continue:** Names your most recently played readable career and jumps right back in.
- **Choose career:** Lets you pick any saved driver. Handy if you want one career for relaxed company driving and another for a manual owner-operator run.
- **Manage careers:** Lets you reset or delete a career. Double-check which driver is selected when the game asks you to confirm.

## Quick start

Here is how your first delivery actually plays out, from creating a driver to reading your first paycheck.

### Choosing how much help you want

On your first launch, pick **All assists**. The truck handles the steering and takes your exits while you learn the rhythm of the road, the sounds, and the readout keys. You can change that anytime under **Settings, Gameplay, Driving assistance**.

### Starting your career

Choose **New career** on the main menu and enter your driver's name. Next, decide whether to drive for a company or start as an owner-operator, then pick your region and home terminal.

If you are just learning the game, starting as a **company driver** is the smoother on-ramp. Your carrier gives you a truck and covers fuel, repairs, and routine upkeep. As an **owner-operator**, those bills come out of your own pocket, so you have to keep cash on hand between loads.

### Choosing your carrier and home terminal

If you drive for a company, your carrier shapes what dispatch sends you and how your pay is calculated. Take a moment to read each description before choosing:

- **Northstar Freight Lines:** A well-rounded starter choice with balanced company wages and a wide mix of freight, without leaning heavily into any one lane.
- **Great Lakes Training Transport:** Built for learning the ropes. It offers more short-haul training runs, stronger stop pay on short loads, and slightly more forgiving deadlines. Once you reach level 4 with full dispatch trust, **Business status** lets you transfer to any of the other three carriers.
- **Prairie Link Regional:** Keeps more of its runs inside the same region and leans toward grain and bulk freight. Its per-mile floor is higher, while stop pay is lower, so watch how much of a job's pay comes from miles versus stops.
- **Summit Value Logistics:** Focuses on long-haul and high-value lanes. It offers a stronger percentage and on-time bonus with a smaller guarantee, so your paycheck depends more on the load's revenue and arriving on time.

No matter which path you pick, you start at level 1. A company driver starts with 5,000 dollars, a full tank, and carrier equipment. A leased-on owner starts with 18,000 dollars and a new truck, along with all of its operating costs. You can keep the suggested home terminal or pick another city.

When you arrive at your terminal, read the first-day briefing. It lays out your first goal and what costs to expect. After you take your first dispatch, **Career plan** tracks your next milestone. From there, each job follows the same steps: pick up freight, haul it safely, read the settlement, and grab your next load. Always leave room in that plan for fuel and legal rest.

### Finding your first load

Open **Dispatch board** at the terminal and accept a load. You do not have the freight yet: your first drive is an empty run over to the shipper to pick it up.

### Getting the truck moving

Here is the startup sequence every time you climb into the cab:

1. Start the engine with **E** (controller: hold **right bumper** and press **A**).
2. Wait a few seconds for air pressure to climb to 100 psi. The brakes need that air before you roll.
3. Release the parking brake with **P** (controller: hold **right bumper** and press **Y**).
4. Hold **Up arrow** to accelerate and **Down arrow** to brake (controller: **right trigger** accelerates and **left trigger** brakes, smoothly based on how far you press).

You do not need to hold the throttle down for the whole highway. Once automatic speed control is available, press **K** (controller: **Y**) to turn it on. On the open highway, **K** runs adaptive cruise; on city streets and facility approaches, it runs speed keeper. Press **Space** (controller: **B**) anytime to hear your speed and which speed-control mode is active.

### Collecting the freight

Follow the guidance to the shipper, stop at the gate, and check in. The yard will either live-load your trailer or point you to a pre-loaded trailer waiting in the lot. If you are picking up a dropped trailer, take a few minutes to walk around it before hooking up so you do not haul somebody else's defect onto the highway.

Once you are loaded, company drivers take the route dispatch assigns, while owner-operators pick their own route. Now you have freight on board, so both the delivery deadline and the cargo's condition count for the rest of the trip.

### Keeping track of the road

While you are rolling, a handful of readout keys tell you everything you need to know without pausing:

- **Space** (controller: **B**): Speed, gear, and speed-control status.
- **R** (controller: **D-pad up**): Where you are and how far you have left.
- **C** (controller: **D-pad right**): The clock, deadline, and your next hours limit (on a controller, D-pad right reads all of your hours limits together).
- **U** (controller: **Driving commands**): What is coming up on the road ahead.
- **V** (controller: **D-pad left**): Current weather and forecast.

On a controller, any readout without its own button lives in **Driving commands**: hold **right bumper**, press **Start**, and pick **Driving commands**.

Do not wait until the tank is dry or your hours are gone to think about stopping. While rolling, press **T** (controller: **right bumper + D-pad down**) to plan a stop for fuel, a break, sleep, or food, and press **X** (controller: **D-pad down**) to signal for its exit. Keep tabs on your legal hours alongside the delivery deadline: **Alt+A** reads time used at the wheel and on duty, **Alt+S** tells you when your 30-minute break is due, and **Alt+D** reads both shift clocks along with reachable legal rest stops.

When a break is due, pull in and take it before pushing on. Do not assume you always have 11 hours of driving ahead of you: your 8-hour break can come due earlier, and your 14-hour duty window can close before you finish 11 hours at the wheel. A short break does not reset a shift. Check [Hours of service and fatigue](#hours-of-service-and-fatigue) whenever you are deciding between a quick break and a full sleep.

### Reaching the receiver

As you near your destination, listen for the exit call. With full lane keeping, the truck takes the exit for you. Otherwise, signal with **X** (controller: **D-pad down**), move into the right lane, and steer into the exit lane once it opens. Slow down before the ramp curve and stop for any traffic light or stop sign at the bottom.

You still have a short stretch of local streets after the highway. Follow the turn calls into the receiver's yard, come to a complete stop, and press **Enter** (controller: **A**) to confirm arrival. Pick **Dock and deliver** to hand over the load.

### Reading your settlement

Take a second to read the settlement before jumping into the next job. It breaks down your earnings, cargo condition, and career changes, including any deductions or fines so you know what helped and what hurt.

Need a breather at any point? **Escape** (controller: **Start**) pauses the drive. Both the main menu and the pause menu give you **Learn game sounds** and **Practice keys and buttons**. And if the music, radio, or outside ambience is drowning out road cues, lower that slider in **Settings, Audio**.

## Menu controls

Every menu tells you its title, the item you are on, and where you are in the list, such as "3 of 10." Press **F1** anytime to hear what the highlighted option does. If you would rather skip the "3 of 10" counts, turn off **Menu position announcements** in Speech settings.

### Menu navigation

Move with **Up** and **Down**, pick with **Enter** or **Space**, and back out with **Escape**. On long menus, you can jump straight to the top or bottom, page ten items at a time, or press a letter or number to hop to a matching entry.

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Up arrow or Down arrow | Move through choices. |
| Enter or Space | Activate the selected choice. |
| Escape | Go back, cancel, or repeat a status message when leaving is not useful. |
| Home or End | Jump to the first or last choice. |
| Page Up or Page Down | Move ten choices back or forward, stopping at the first or last. |
| Letter or number | Jump to the next choice starting with that character. |
| F1 | Hear help for the current item. |
| Comma | Review earlier speech, except while typing text. |
| Period | Move toward newer speech, except while typing text. |

On a controller:

| Button | Action |
| --- | --- |
| D-pad up or down | Move through choices. |
| D-pad left or right | Change the value of a setting. |
| A | Activate the selected choice. |
| B | Go back. |
| Back | Read help for the selected choice, just like F1. |

Controllers do not have buttons for jumping to the ends of a list or for message review; reach for the keyboard when you want those.

### Reviewing what the game said

Missed an announcement over the engine or traffic? Message review lets you step back through recent speech without losing your place.

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Comma | Repeat what was just said, then keep stepping back. |
| Period | Move toward newer messages. |
| Ctrl+Comma | Jump to the oldest message kept. |
| Ctrl+Period | Jump back to the newest message. |
| Left bracket or right bracket | Switch between all messages, general messages, and driving events. |
| Ctrl+C | Copy the message you are currently reviewing to the clipboard. |
| Ctrl+Shift+C | Copy the newest line in message history to the clipboard from any screen (a heading, status result, call, or driving line; menu positions like "2 of 8" are left out). Rebindable under Settings, Controls. |

Message review holds the last 200 messages, skipping routine menu movement. New announcements will not yank you away from the line you are reading. After ten seconds without pressing a review key, the next press starts from the newest message again while keeping your chosen category. While you are typing in a text field, Comma and Period type normally instead of opening review.

### How to play controls

Press either **Control** key or **F1** to stop speech mid-sentence, and **Escape** to return to the menu you came from.

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Left arrow or Page Up | Previous help page. |
| Right arrow or Page Down | Next help page. |
| Up arrow or Down arrow | Read the previous or next part of the page. |
| Enter or Space | Read the whole current page. |
| Either Control key or F1 | Stop speech. |
| Escape | Return to the previous menu. |

### Entering text

When the game asks you to type a name or address, **Backspace** deletes a character, **F2** reads back what you have typed, **Enter** confirms, **Escape** cancels, and **Ctrl+V** pastes from the clipboard.

A controller cannot type. On the driver-name screen, pressing **A** without typing names your driver "Driver," and **B** cancels.

## Terminal and garage

Between loads, you are parked at the terminal. This is your home base for checking your money, reputation, and logbook, getting a full night's sleep, booking training, servicing the truck, and picking up your next dispatch.

| Choice | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Dispatch board | Browse freight offers from local facilities. |
| Truck dealer | Owner-operators only. Browse tractors at the local dealer, buy one, or switch between trucks you own. The intro names a local dealer when one is listed for that area. |
| Drive to a nearby city without a trailer | Owner-operators only. Deadhead empty to a nearby city to check its dispatch board. Uses fuel and legal hours, with no load and no pay. |
| Career plan | Check your next career milestone after your first dispatch. |
| Business status | Review your company-driver or owner-operator standing and next business step. |
| Garage | Refuel, repair, service tires, brakes, and engine, buy snow chains, and wash the truck. Owner-operators can also fit winter tires, buy upgrades, buy or switch tractors, add trailer programs, or buy trailers once they have own authority. |
| Request pay advance | Draw cash against your next load when your wallet is empty. |
| Career stats | Check your level, reputation, deliveries, career totals, endorsements, dispatch trust, safety record, CDL status, and any debt. Choose **Citations and violations** to read them newest first: what happened, why, what it cost, when, and where. |
| Licenses and training | Book any credential course, from manual transmission training to the LCV certificate. Press F1 on any row to hear what it unlocks and when the carrier sponsors it versus when you can pay early. |
| Truck status | Check your tractor model, fuel, tank size, damage and damage band, tire wear, and road grime. |
| Walk around the truck | A 15-minute on-duty pre-trip inspection that catches defects a roadside inspector would spot on the tractor. |
| Time and weather | Check the clock, date, season, career day, and local weather, including how old a live weather report is when available. |
| Logbook | Review your Record of Duty Status: current duty, hours limits, today's totals, and recent entries. |
| Sleep 10 hours | Rest at the terminal and reset your hours of service. |
| Save game | Save your current career. |
| Settings | Open the settings categories. |
| Quit to main menu | Save and return to the title menu. |

### Looking after the truck

Before you grab another load, take a quick look at **Truck status** to see where your fuel, damage, tire wear, and grime sit. Running **Walk around the truck** spends 15 on-duty minutes checking the tractor for anything a roadside inspector would flag. If something needs attention, head into **Garage** before you roll out.

Inside **Garage** you can fuel up, repair damage, replace worn tires, reline brakes, overhaul the engine, buy snow chains, and wash the truck. Company drivers get carrier-paid routine equipment care. Owner-operators pay out of pocket, so always make sure you leave enough cash for fuel and service between settlements.

Owners can also choose tire compounds and install upgrades for torque, aerodynamics, fuel capacity, and stronger brakes. Match your upgrades to the lanes you run: a bigger tank pays off on long western stretches, while reinforced brakes help most in the mountains. Check your truck and business status before spending money you will need on the next run.

### Choosing equipment and work

As an owner-operator, **Truck dealer** lets you shop for tractors, buy a new one, or swap between trucks you already own. **Garage** also gives you access to your owned trucks and trailer programs. Adding a specialty program opens new kinds of freight; once you reach own authority, you can buy your own trailers outright. See [Business status](#business-status) for how the progression and costs work.

If a local dispatch board looks dry, **Drive to a nearby city without a trailer** lets an owner deadhead to a neighboring city's board. You still burn fuel and legal hours with no paycheck for the move, so make sure the better freight is worth the empty miles.

### Licenses and training

See a locked load on the board? Open **Licenses and training** to qualify for it. Some certificates are sponsored by your carrier once you reach the right level, or you can pay out of pocket to earn them a level early. Endorsements and port cards unlock specialized freight. **Hazmat** and **TWIC** require background checks that take game days to clear, so apply ahead of time. Bulk fuel requires both tank vehicle and hazmat endorsements together (the X combination). Training also uses time, so check your hours and fatigue before heading straight out on a run afterward.

| Credential | Unlocks | How you get it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manual transmission training | A manual-spec pay differential on drives made with a manual gearbox. | Course only, 220 dollars from level 1. |
| Refrigerated certificate | Fresh food and refrigerated goods. | Free from the carrier at level 2, or 900 dollars from level 1. |
| Flatbed securement certificate | Steel products, plus lumber and paper products. | Free from the carrier at level 2, or 450 dollars from level 1. |
| Heavy-haul certificate | Heavy machinery. | Free from the carrier at level 3, or 1,600 dollars from level 2. |
| High-value certificate | Electronics and packaged industrial chemicals. | Free from the carrier at level 4, or 1,300 dollars from level 3. |
| Doubles endorsement | Twin-trailer parcel freight. | Course only, 60 dollars from level 8. |
| Hazmat endorsement | Placarded hazardous materials, and bulk fuel once paired with the tank vehicle endorsement. | Course only, 185 dollars from level 10, plus a background check of about 30 days. |
| Tank vehicle endorsement | Liquid food products from level 21, and bulk fuel once paired with the hazmat endorsement. | Free from the carrier at level 16, or 2,400 dollars from level 15. |
| TWIC port card | Port containers. | Course only, 125 dollars from level 18, plus a background check of about 20 days. |
| LCV certificate | Turnpike doubles freight between states whose networks allow them. | Course only, 2,000 dollars from level 20. Requires the doubles endorsement and a clean recent record. |

## Business status

Your career spans 30 levels, and you decide whether to climb the ladder as a company driver or branch out on your own.

### Company driving and carrier fleets

As a company driver, you earn wages and bonuses based on what the carrier makes, and the carrier supplies the truck and pays for routine upkeep. Each carrier sets its own wage floor, stop pay, pay share, bonuses, deadlines, and freight mix.

You never buy a truck as a company driver. Instead, dispatch moves you up through better carrier fleets as you level up, at no cost to you:

- **Level 1:** Yard standard truck.
- **Level 4:** Regional fleet.
- **Level 9:** Long-haul fleet (from here on, you keep one assigned tractor for every load instead of switching spares).
- **Level 13:** Premium fleet.
- **Level 17:** First pick of the yard.

Before level 9, dispatch matches a spare truck to each load (a sleeper for a long run, a heavy-spec tractor for heavy freight, or a day cab for a short turn), and each spare keeps its own fuel, wear, and damage. **Career stats** always tells you which fleet you are in and when the next one opens.

### Becoming an owner-operator and own authority

Want to run your own iron? The **owner-operator buy-in** opens at **level 18**, provided you have at least 35 deliveries, a reputation of 80, no unpaid advance, and enough cash to cover the 35,000-dollar buy-in while still keeping 10,000 dollars in working capital. **Business status** lists whichever requirements you still need.

If you do not want to own a truck, choose **Stay a company driver** to switch your goals and rank titles to the company track. You can always bring the ownership track back later with **Reopen the owner-operator plan**.

Already an owner and want to go back to company driving? Choosing **Go back to company driving** asks you to confirm twice before selling anything. The game buys back your tractors and trailers at the stated values (your buy-in tractor returns its full purchase price; later tractors sell at used value) and puts you back in carrier equipment. Your driving record comes with you.

Owning a truck means paying for fuel, repairs, maintenance and insurance reserves, trailer costs, truck reserves, and fees. A bigger gross payout is not all take-home pay.

Further up the road, **authority preparation** opens at **level 21**, and **own authority** opens at **level 25** once you have 75 deliveries, a reputation of 92, at least one specialty program, no unpaid advance, and the startup cash plus working capital. Direct freight pays higher gross rates, with deductions for insurance, compliance, trailers, trucks, and billing fees. Once you own a trailer that matches the freight, its reserve replaces the trailer program charge.

Owners browse and switch tractors at the **Truck dealer**. Upgrades apply across your whole fleet, while wear, damage, fuel, and winter equipment stay with each individual tractor.

| Upgrade, program, or trailer | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Engine tune | Adds pulling power. |
| Aerodynamic kit | Improves highway fuel economy. |
| Long-range tank | Adds 50 gallons of fuel capacity. |
| Reinforced brakes | Helps the service brakes resist fade longer. |
| Reefer trailer program | Opens refrigerated and fresh food cargo for owner-operators. |
| Flatbed trailer program | Opens steel, machinery, construction, lumber, and paper cargo for owner-operators. |
| Bulk trailer program | Opens grain, farm inputs, and loose bulk cargo for owner-operators. |
| Owned trailer | Own-authority drivers can buy dry van, reefer, flatbed, or bulk trailers. Matching direct freight uses an owned-trailer reserve at settlement. |

### Dispatch trust

Dispatch watches your reputation, driving record, CDL status, and debt when deciding how much freedom and equipment to give you.

| Trust | What dispatch does |
| --- | --- |
| Full | You see the whole board. |
| Guarded | Some freight is held back, and you get fewer refusals. |
| Poor | Assigned loads no matter your level, a board trimmed to two offers, and the best freight goes to other drivers. |
| Last chance | One assigned load at a time, no refusals, and the carrier is deciding whether to keep you. |

Low trust can also cut experience gains or hold back higher-tier trucks. **Career stats** and **Driver status** tell you why a restriction is active and what it takes to clear it; held equipment comes back once the hold lifts. If a company driver's reputation hits rock bottom, Great Lakes Training Transport takes over your contract. Once you climb back to full trust at level 4 or higher, **Business status** lets you transfer to another carrier.

If you run out of cash, you can request a capped **pay advance** against your next load at the terminal, or against your current load at supported road stops. It is deducted at settlement, so treat it as borrowing from your next check. Dispatch will not grant another advance while settlements are already paying down a balance owed.

## Dispatch and jobs

Early in a company career, dispatch assigns your loads. You can turn one down, but each refusal costs reputation and uses up one of your limited refusals. Once you hit **level 8** (assuming your trust is in good shape), you get to pick from the board yourself. Even then, the company still sets your route; owner-operators choose their own routes.

Before you say yes to any load, press **F1** (controller: **Back**) to read the full job sheet. It covers cargo, weight and gross-weight margin, facilities, distance, gross pay, estimated take-home, deadline, and required equipment. Diesel adds weight too. The market note tells you whether rates for that cargo are tight or loose, and the deadline details explain how much break and sleep time is built into the schedule.

Your carrier provides trailers for any freight you are cleared to haul. Owner-operators start with dry van access and add specialty programs as they grow. If a job is locked, its description tells you exactly what is missing. Direct freight also notes when an owned trailer matches the load and lowers your charges.

A destination city is a whole metro freight area, not a single dock; you might pull into a port, warehouse, farm, rail yard, or manufacturing plant. Some approaches follow mapped city streets, while representative locations use the guidance available for them.

Every delivery earns experience for service and mileage, plus bonuses for undamaged cargo, specialty freight, and on-time streaks. Refrigerated freight opens at level 2, heavy haul at level 3, and high-value freight at level 4. As you level up, routes stretch longer, boards grow wider, and new equipment and business paths open up.

Protect your record along the way. A citation costs 4 reputation points for 30 game days. A serious violation or a crash costs 10 points for 90 game days, and a major offense costs 20 points permanently. Record reviews and insurance look back 30 game days for citations and 90 game days for serious violations; CDL suspension rules look back 180 game days for serious violations.

## Pickup, loading, and route planning

Every job starts with an empty drive to the shipper. Pull up to the gate, stop, and check in. That takes 15 on-duty minutes. Live loading takes around 60 minutes of game time, which passes in a quick wait.

### Drop and hook, and the walk-around

If your load is already staged in a dropped trailer, hooking up takes about 25 minutes. Before you couple to it, choose **Walk around the trailer** to inspect its lamps, brakes, and tires. If you find a defect and refuse the trailer, the yard takes about half an hour to swap it out. Hook a bad trailer anyway and you carry that defect straight into the next weigh station or inspection.

Long waits at a facility can pay detention after two hours; the job sheet lists the rate. At a receiver drop yard, dropping and hooking takes about 20 minutes compared with roughly 45 minutes at a live dock. If you are pulling your own trailer, it stays with you, so you will unload at the dock.

### Choosing your route

Once the trailer is loaded, company drivers roll out on dispatch's route. Owner-operators get to compare the available routes first. Look past raw mileage and check legal rest stops, fuel and sleep options, tolls, mountain grades, and parking availability. Press **W** to check weather along a route before committing. Once you are rolling, live weather follows your truck's real-world location, while simulated weather follows the forecast.

## Driving controls

Below are the default keyboard shortcuts with their default controller buttons alongside. You can rebind most controls under **Settings, Gameplay, Controls**, and in-game help will always speak your custom keys. Menu navigation, message review, and a few core keys stay fixed.

Want to test what a key does without hitting it on the highway? Press **Shift+F1** anytime for [key and button practice](#practice-keys-and-buttons).

This manual assumes the default bumper layout (right bumper for the second layer, left bumper for the clutch). If you flip **Second layer bumper** in Controls settings, those two bumpers trade places and the game names them accordingly.

Whenever a row says **Driving commands**, that action has no dedicated controller button. Hold **right bumper** and press **Start** to open Driving status, choose **Driving commands**, and pick the action from the list. It runs right away and puts you back on the road.

| Key | Controller | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up arrow, hold | Right trigger, as far as you press it | Throttle. |
| Down arrow, hold | Left trigger, as far as you press it | Brake. To put an automatic in reverse: stop completely, let go of Down, then press and hold Down again for a moment. A quick tap, or holding brake through the stop, only brakes. Do the same release-and-hold on Up arrow to shift back to forward. |
| Down arrow, tap then press and hold | Not on the controller | Latch the brake hands-free. After about half a second, a click and spoken line confirm it is latched. ### Manual transmission controls

When you drive a manual, hold the clutch (**Shift** or **left bumper**) before shifting, or you will hear the gears grind. Shifting up from neutral or reverse puts you in first gear. (Also, **Alt** plus keypad 1 through 5 works the same as **Alt+1** through **Alt+5** for quick location checks.)

| Key | Controller | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Left Shift or Right Shift, hold | Left bumper, hold | Clutch. |
| W | A | Shift up a gear (from neutral or reverse, selects first gear). |
| Q | X | Shift down a gear. |
| N | X from first gear | Neutral. |
| Backspace | X from neutral | Reverse. |

On a controller, **A** and **X** step through gears in sequence: pressing **X** from first goes to neutral, pressing **X** again goes to reverse, and **A** climbs back up the same way.

### Controller controls

You can use a controller right alongside your keyboard at any time. Button names here follow the Xbox layout. In menus, move with the **D-pad**, select with **A**, and go back with **B**. **Back** reads help (at the wheel, it cuts off driving speech if something is playing, and reads help otherwise). **Start** pauses.

On the road, **right trigger** is throttle and **left trigger** is brake; pulling the left trigger all the way down engages the emergency brake. The **left stick** steers; with full lane keeping on, push it left or right to change one lane, then let it return to center before requesting another. To switch an automatic between drive and reverse, stop completely and give the opposite trigger a fresh hold. **Left bumper** is the clutch, and **right bumper** activates the second layer of button bindings.

Most actions have a direct button. For the remaining readouts (such as grade, lane check, and road ahead), hold **right bumper** and press **Start**, then open **Driving commands** and pick what you want.

To check what any button does on the fly, hold **Back** and press **Start** for key and button practice. Press any button, trigger, or stick to hear its job, then press **Start** twice to get back to driving.

Inside **How to play**, **D-pad left/right** turns pages, **D-pad up/down** reads a part at a time, **A** reads the whole page, **Back** stops speech, and **B** exits.

## Truck behavior

A semi does not move like a passenger car, and a few real-world truck habits will save you a lot of trouble.

### Air pressure, weight, and the parking brake

After starting the engine, wait for air pressure to build to 100 psi before releasing the brakes. Repeated hard braking drains that air supply; if it drops low, pull over somewhere safe and let the compressor rebuild it. **Tab** (detailed status) reads the primary, secondary, and trailer air tanks individually.

A heavy load takes noticeably longer to get up to speed. The automatic transmission shifts for the load and grade on its own, while the manual gives you ten forward gears to manage yourself.

Watch your gross weight when hauling near the limit. Fuel is heavy, and the fuel menu shows your projected gross weight before you top off. A **CAT Scale** checks your individual axle groups too.

Only set the parking brake (**P**, or **right bumper + Y**) once you are completely stopped. Pulling it at speed slams the spring brakes on and grinds permanent flat spots into the tires. While parked with the parking brake set, pressing **K** (controller: **Y**) latches high idle until you release the brake.

### Automatic speed control and cruise

On the open highway at 20 miles per hour or faster, **K** (controller: **Y**) starts **adaptive cruise**. On city streets and facility approaches, **K** starts **speed keeper** instead. Cruise automatically respects work zones and heavy traffic limits, follows vehicles ahead, and opens up a wider gap in bad weather.

Adjust your cruise target with **Plus** and **Minus** (controller: **right bumper + D-pad right or left**), which snaps outward to the next multiple of 5 mph: from 32, Plus jumps to 35, then 40. Hold **Ctrl** with Plus or Minus to nudge it 1 mph at a time. Press **Space** to hear which mode is active, what speed is set, and whether road or traffic conditions are holding you below it.

Touching the brake cancels speed control; **Shift+K** resumes at your last target. At a planned pickup, speed control pauses through loading and saving, then picks right back up once you roll out loaded.

### Engine brake

Press **J** (controller: **right stick click**) to toggle the engine brake, and use **1**, **2**, or **3** (controller: **right bumper + right stick click** to step) to choose two, four, or six cylinders of braking while it is on. A lower gear and higher RPM give the engine brake more holding power. Press **Alt+J** in an automatic to toggle between automatic stage management and picking stages yourself.

Listen for town noise restrictions: the game warns you before writing a ticket for using the engine brake in a restricted zone, with exceptions for downhill grades and emergencies. When you clear a restricted area, you will hear "End of no engine brake zone."

## Truck damage and being out of service

Stay ahead of wear before it strands you on the shoulder. Worn tires lose grip, worn brakes fade faster on grades, and a worn engine loses pulling power. Each tractor tracks its own wear, damage, fuel, and winter gear. Plan a garage visit when any component hits **80 percent wear**. At **100 percent**, that component puts the truck out of service. The garage can replace tires, reline brakes, and overhaul engines.

| Damage | What the truck does |
| --- | --- |
| Under 50 percent | Normal power with no damage penalty. |
| Past 50 percent | **Reduced power.** The engine holds back and burns more fuel for the same work. |
| Past 75 percent | **Limp mode.** Reduced power plus a 45 miles per hour speed cap you cannot drive past. The cap eases in over a few seconds rather than snapping on. |
| Past 85 percent | A spoken last call naming the out-of-service threshold before you cross it. |
| Past 90 percent | **Out of service.** You can no longer continue the run under your own power. The truck can still crawl clear of a live lane so you are not stranded in traffic, but you need roadside help to go on. |

The damage readout gives both the percentage and the current band.

Whenever something damages the truck on the road, a spoken line tells you what happened and your current damage, for example, "Off the road on the right! Damage 1 percent." While you stay off the pavement, further hits are signaled by the rumble strip alone, and once you are back on asphalt you hear the updated total: "Back on the pavement. Damage 3 percent." Pushing past 90 miles per hour puts the truck into a runaway that deals damage every second. You hear "Runaway! The truck is taking damage." when it begins, and "Back under control." with the damage total once you drop a few miles per hour below 90.

If you break down on the road, owner-operators pay for roadside repairs, lose several hours, and still need proper garage service afterward. Company drivers get carrier-paid service and a yard spare, though preventable equipment trouble still dings your record and dispatch trust.

Preventable damage can also cost your safety bonus and add a deductible, and fixing it on the road does not erase that hit, because settlement scores the worst damage band reached during the trip. Correct reactions to road hazards do not count as preventable damage. Visible unsafe equipment can also draw a roadside inspection. For company drivers, any tractor at 40 percent damage or higher automatically goes through the carrier shop when you accept your next load, at no charge and one hour on duty.

## Mountain driving

Hills are won before you crest them. Press **G** (controller: **Driving commands**) to hear the current grade, its length, how the truck is handling it, and the next hill worth preparing for. Sustained grades of three percent over three quarters of a mile are normally announced ahead. If you use **Quiet** or **Urgent only** driving speech, check grades yourself with **G**.

Get your speed and gear set before you tip over the top of a downgrade. Let the engine brake hold a steady pace, and use the service brakes in short, firm applications with time to cool in between. Riding the service brakes heats them up and causes fade. If you hear hot-brake warnings, slow down and let them cool right away.

Press **D** anytime for a safe descent speed tailored to your truck and load. If descent control and cruise are on, they coordinate speed, gear, engine braking, and short service-brake taps for you. Just be careful on ice: a heavy engine-brake setting can break your drive tires loose.

**Predictive cruise** scans ahead for hills: it builds momentum for a climb, lets speed bleed slightly over the crest, and holds back from accelerating right before a descent. If a climb is too steep to hold your set speed, it warns you.

## Taking curves

Brake down to the advisory speed before you reach the curve, not halfway through it. Curve calls give you direction, distance, and advisory speed with enough room to slow down. Press **U** to review upcoming bends and **D** for your current safe speed. When two tight bends come back to back, slow to the lower advisory.

### Steering and road sounds

With the default steering guide, steer **toward** the side the engine leans.

- **Lane keeping and curve assistance both off:** Hold toward the called turn to follow the road around the bend.
- **Partial lane keeping or curve assistance on:** The truck tracks the curve while you correct drift.
- **Full lane keeping on:** The truck steers for you; arrow taps (or left-stick pushes) request lane changes.

There are two sound cues: the **steering guide** tells you which way to steer, while **road noise** tells you where the truck is sitting. Road noise on the right means you are drifting right of center. You can reverse the steering-guide direction in **Settings, Audio**, or switch it from the engine to a soft tone with **Lane guide sound**. Press **I** to toggle a steady lane-position tock whenever lane keeping is on partial or off.

Listen for the road-edge progression: warning-bar thuds before a hairpin, a side-marker thump when a tire crosses the line, then rumble-strip stutter, buzz, and gravel as you drift farther off. Ease back gently. Slamming the brakes with trailer wheels in dirt makes recovery harder.

On undivided two-lane roads, crossing the centerline puts you into oncoming traffic. At the end of a bend, the verdict tells you whether you held your line, caught the edge, or came in too hot.

## Winter driving

Snow, ice, and freezing rain cut your grip, especially on worn tires. Steer and brake smoothly, and go easy on the engine brake; a strong setting can break the drive tires loose on slick pavement.

### Winter tires and snow chains

| Equipment | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Winter tires | A fresh set in a winter compound. Gives better grip on snow and ice all season with normal wear and no speed cap. Company drivers run whatever the carrier equips. |
| Snow chains | Carried in the truck's side box until you hang them. Gives the best grip on ice and packed snow, but requires chain speed (around 30 miles per hour) and grinds apart quickly on bare pavement. Snapped chains have to be replaced at the garage. |

To hang or remove chains, come to a full stop and open the pause menu. The work logs as on-duty time, and doing it in the dark takes longer and adds fatigue. While chains are on, stay near 30 miles per hour, and pull over to take them off once you hit bare road before they snap.

### Chain laws

Chain laws apply only on steep grades while snow or ice is on the road in nine states: California, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nevada, Oregon, Utah, Washington, and Wyoming.

- **Level 1:** Chains are required on your truck in California, Idaho, Montana, Nevada, Utah, and Washington. Wyoming accepts winter tires or chains. Colorado and Oregon apply Level 1 only to single-drive-axle trucks, so your tandem-axle truck passes without chains.
- **Level 2:** Chains are required in all nine states.

You will hear "Chain law lifted" when the road clears, and "Bare road" if your chains are still mounted with no snow under them. A co-driver at the wheel does the chain work themselves: chains on at the chain-up area before the control, and off again on bare road. If snow starts while you are already on the grade, the control does not cite you for that stretch. Running past an active chain law carries a 580-dollar base citation before multipliers.

## Traffic and sharing the road

Traffic around you slows, merges, passes, and backs up into queues. Build in extra buffer for rush hour around busy metros, and ease off early when you are closing on slower vehicles ahead.

### Following and passing

Before you move over, press **L** (controller: **Driving commands, Lane position**) to check your lane and the space beside you, and listen for clearance before pulling back in after a pass. Nearby traffic sounds alert you that someone is there, but they never replace a proper lane check; a car can sit quietly beside your trailer while your mind is on an exit or curve.

**Adaptive cruise** paces traffic ahead and stretches your following gap in bad weather, though it does not steer. With **full lane keeping** on, the truck can pass slow traffic on its own and return when clear (it will not start a pass within two miles of an exit), and it moves over for road debris or a car stopped on the shoulder when an adjacent lane is open. If you missed the clearance call, press **L** to check again.

### Listening to nearby vehicles

A passing vehicle builds in volume as it pulls alongside and fades as it moves ahead, panning across the stereo field so you hear which side it is on. At controlled ramp intersections, cross traffic moves across the sound field. Stop for the control and wait for a clear gap before pulling out.

On an on-ramp, mainline highway traffic stays to your left and fades as the ramp separates. Distant freeway sound also reflects real traffic volume, so a packed commuter belt sounds very different from a quiet highway at 2 AM. Open **Learn game sounds** anytime if you want to hear a cue by itself before meeting it in traffic.

### Traffic reports and hazards

Check the [Traffic app](#traffic-app) or listen to GPS calls for slowdowns ahead, though a clear report never guarantees every lane is empty. Keep checking with **L**.

When the game calls **Brake now**, hit the brakes right away; some hazards require dropping below 25 miles per hour. A **Change lanes or brake** call names an open lane. Take it if you can. When no lane is open:

- **Ladders, lumber, mattresses, or spilled boxes:** Survivable with no damage at 30 miles per hour or under; faster than that costs damage.
- **Rockfalls or a spun-out car ahead:** Require a full stop.
- **Tire debris:** Called with the open lane; nothing brakes for it, and running over it at highway speed can occasionally cost a point or two of damage.

See [Construction and exits](#construction-and-exits) for work-zone lane closures and leaving the highway.

## Road events, weather, and rest stops

Your GPS announces state lines, towns, route turns, work zones, tolls, and upcoming stops. Stay alert for lane closures and your planned exit.

### Construction and exits

Slow down as work-zone speed limits step down, and merge out of a closing lane when told. With full lane keeping, the truck moves over when the signs start and waits behind traffic for a gap if needed. Pushing past the urgent barrel warning damages the truck and triggers a 1,000-dollar citation plus a serious violation (charged once per zone, not doubled again for construction). If the highway itself drops a lane and repositions you, there is no penalty.

To plan a stop while rolling, press **T** (controller: **right bumper + D-pad down**) and pick what you need: fuel, a break, sleep, fuel and sleep, or food and coffee. Each option reads the distance to its stop, and **Enter** locks it in. With planning hints on, a break or sleep plan selects the recommended stop; when your tank is low or will not reach the next pumps, the menu opens directly on fuel. Press **T** again to cancel the plan.

Planning a stop does **not** signal for its exit. Press **X** (controller: **D-pad down**) to set or cancel the exit signal separately. The call names the exit and its ramp control, and the turn signal starts clicking about half a mile out.

With partial lane keeping or lane keeping off, move into the right lane and steer into the exit lane once it opens. Hold highway speed until you are in the exit lane, then brake down to exit speed before the ramp curve. Full lane keeping handles both the lane moves and the destination exit.

Missed your destination exit? Do not force a move across a blocked lane. Keep rolling and follow the next safe turnaround guidance.

### Ramp ends and gates

At a ramp traffic light, stop on red. On yellow, stop if you safely can (entering on yellow is allowed). At a stop sign, come to a complete stop and wait for a clear gap. Listen for the sensor tick speeding up as you near the stop bar. The game clock runs in real time through controlled ramp intersections.

Once clear, pull forward as directed. **Facility stopping assistance** can manage the pedals all the way to the gate while **speed keeper** handles the city streets in between; pressing your brake cancels pedal assistance whenever you want to take over.

Local streets have their own speed limits and corner calls. Each turn is called with its distance and target speed, accompanied by a chime on the side you are turning. Take a corner more than about 3 miles per hour over that speed and you miss the turn: the truck loops around the block and brings you back onto the street a few minutes later. Traffic controls are not modeled on every side street. A yard driveway enters at 15 miles per hour; where there is no driveway, the gate sits right on the street.

### Stop services

Once you come to a full stop at a supported location, press **T** (controller: **right bumper + D-pad down**) to open its services menu. Depending on the stop, you can buy fuel or food, shower, take a break, sleep, arrange repairs, weigh at a CAT Scale, walk around the truck, check in for inspection, call roadside help, or save. Public rest areas often lack fuel and repairs. Even when a truck stop's parking lot is full, you can usually still buy diesel.

A meal or coffee takes the edge off fatigue for a while, and a 30-minute meal also satisfies your legal break. Fueling can earn a free shower for that visit. Food and showers come out of your own pocket, while your carrier covers equipment care. Some stops also offer loyalty rewards you can spend on-site.

A **CAT Scale** weigh takes 10 on-duty minutes and reads your steer, drive, trailer, and gross weights. Legal limits are 34,000 pounds on a tandem and 80,000 pounds gross. The first weigh costs 15 dollars and 25 cents; a reweigh at the same scale within 24 hours costs 5 dollars and 25 cents. Company drivers bill it to the carrier.

When an **open weigh station** is called, slow into the scale lane, signal with **X** (controller: **D-pad down**), stop, and press **T** (controller: **right bumper + D-pad down**) to check in. A required scale always takes priority over a sleep plan.

Do not let the tank run dry. Warnings fire near 15 percent fuel (naming the next fuel stop) and whenever your range will not reach the next pumps or destination. Truck stops, fuel stations, service plazas, and parking lots announce **Fuel** or **No fuel** as they come up. Running out costs an owner-operator a rescue bill and gives a company driver a service-record hit.

### Weather and calendar

Press **V** (controller: **D-pad left**) to read the weather and forecast. Weather affects grip, visibility, safe speed, cruise gap, drag, fuel burn, and road sound. Every career starts on March 21 and moves through the seasons as you drive and rest. Check the date with **C**, in status screens, or at the terminal.

By default, live weather drives the calendar. Turn off **Live weather controls calendar** if you want the career date to advance independently; an existing career starts its independent calendar on today's date with weather adjusted to that season. At a terminal, temperature comes from a live weather station when available.

### Time zones

In accelerated modes, the game speaks the truck's local time and announces time-zone crossings. Delivery appointments always use the receiver's local time and include the day. Real time follows your computer's clock without changing the time left on a load or your hours-of-service clocks. Daylight saving time is not modeled.

## Enforcement, your record, and your CDL

Troopers and inspectors cite violations they observe. The dash alert that chimes when you are more than 7 miles per hour over the limit is a free warning; brake down and it quiets with no fine. When a speed limit drops, get off the throttle and slow toward the new limit during the grace allowance.

If a trooper lights you up, press **X** (controller: **D-pad down**) and brake to a stop for the license and logbook check. Ignoring the lights brings escalating warnings, and merely slowing down can end in a forced stop plus a failure-to-stop citation.

After the final warning, twelve seconds of highway-speed driving or accelerating without braking triggers a pursuit. Fleeing can damage your truck, cancel the load, burn time in processing, and put a major offense on your record.

CB chatter shares what other drivers spotted and how sure they were. Treat it as a tip, not a certainty, since patrols move. Press **Alt+C** to repeat the last CB report with an updated distance, and **U** for fixed road features.

### What a citation costs

While you are below **level 10**, officers let you off with a warning for most tickets: no fine, no reputation loss, and nothing on your record. The big ones are still cited even early on: speeding 15 or more over, driving through construction barrels, failing to pull over, running an open scale, and anything that puts you out of service.

| Citation | Base fine |
| --- | --- |
| Unsafe equipment | 2,300 dollars |
| Running an open scale | 1,800 dollars |
| Failing to pull over promptly | 1,500 dollars |
| Driving through the barrels | 1,000 dollars |
| Following too closely | 600 dollars |
| Chain law | 580 dollars |
| Improper lane use | 500 dollars |

Speeding base fines step up by how far over you are: 250 dollars under 10 over, 400 from 10 over, 1,000 from 15 over, 1,600 from 20 over, and 2,500 from 30 over. Fleeing starts at 5,000 dollars. Work zones double applicable fines (excluding the barrel citation).

Repeat offenses stack a record multiplier too: each step adds half the base fine up to double. For example, bypassing an open scale costs 1,800 dollars the first time, 2,700 the next, then 3,600. Work-zone and record multipliers combine, so repeat 30-over speeding in construction can hit 10,000 dollars.

### Inspections and CDL suspension

Recent tickets and out-of-service orders draw more inspections.

- **Level 1 inspection:** Checks paperwork and equipment; takes about 45 on-duty minutes. Critical defects hold you on-site for repair. Passing earns a clean inspection decal that normally lasts three months unless your record draws extra attention.
- **Level 3 inspection:** Checks your license and paperwork; takes about 15 minutes.

Roadcheck week in May raises inspection rates across the board. Running a 15-minute walk-around before leaving the terminal catches defects before an inspector does.

**Serious violations** include speeding 15 or more over, reckless driving, unsafe lane changes, tailgating, failing to stop, hitting construction barrels, and repeated fatigued driving. Two serious violations within 180 game days suspend your CDL for **60 days**; three or more suspend it for **120 days**, and suspensions run back to back. Fleeing disqualifies your CDL for **one year** the first time and **for life** the second.

While suspended, you cannot haul freight, and any active or saved drive ends. You keep your level, experience, endorsements, and equipment. Check **Career stats** or **Driver status** to see your current standing and what one more violation would trigger.

## When you owe money

If a fine, repair, or claim costs more than the cash in your account, the shortfall becomes a balance owed. Going forward, one quarter of each settlement pays down debt and advances together, leaving you three quarters as take-home. You cannot take a new advance while debt is being collected. Check **Career stats** or **Driver status** anytime to hear your balance, your debt ceiling, and what happens if you hit it.

You get warnings when debt starts, when it crosses halfway, and when it nears the ceiling:

- **Company driver ceiling:** Eight average settlements or 6,000 dollars, whichever is higher.
- **Owner-operator ceiling:** Sixty percent of tractor resale value or 12,000 dollars, whichever is higher.

Hit the ceiling and the lender or carrier steps in. A company driver loses their carrier job and moves to Great Lakes Training Transport. An owner-operator's lender repossesses the tractors and returns the driver to company work. Your debt zeroes out along with your cash, while you keep your level, experience, endorsements, driving record, and other belongings. If you are already at Great Lakes, debt stops growing at the ceiling; anything over is written off while repayments continue, and a report summarizes the outcome.

## Hours of service and fatigue

Managing your clocks and your energy is half the job of trucking.

### Legal hours of service

Your legal limits are the same in both hours modes; **Relaxed** eases enforcement pressure, it does not add hours to your clock:

- **11-hour driving limit:** Up to 11 hours behind the wheel after 10 hours off duty.
- **14-hour duty window:** All driving must happen within 14 hours of starting your shift.
- **30-minute break:** Required after 8 cumulative hours of driving.

Any 30 consecutive minutes not driving satisfies the break (loading and inspections count), though they do not pause or reset the 14-hour duty window. Moving the truck for work counts as driving even without a trailer.

Check your clocks before committing to the next stretch:

- **Alt+A:** Time driven and on duty so far this shift.
- **Alt+S:** When your 30-minute break is due (or if a break will not save your shift).
- **Alt+D:** Both shift clocks plus reachable legal rest stops.
- **C** (controller: **D-pad right** reads the clock and all three limits together): Clock, deadline, and nearest limit.

Before each limit runs out, you get a warning: 1 hour out in Relaxed driving mode, 2 hours out in Standard, and at 2 hours, 1 hour, and 30 minutes in Real time (no warning if your delivery ends before the limit). When you depart with a load, the departure line summarizes your driving time left, fuel, whether you need a break or sleep, and how much of the run falls at night between 9 PM and 5 AM if you drive straight through (for example, "About 4 hours of night driving"; daytime runs omit night).

A 30-minute break will not fix an expiring shift, and slow traffic or bad weather can push a planned stop out of reach. Recheck reachability whenever conditions slow you down.

### Sleeping and sleeper-berth splits

Ten hours of sleep resets your legal hours. At supported parking, you can also split rest in the sleeper berth: **8+2** or **7+3** hours (choosing 2, 3, 7, or 8 hours in the berth). The long half pauses your 14-hour duty window. The short half counts against the window until you complete the matching half, at which point the game recalculates duty between rests. If a nap leaves your duty window ticking, the menu tells you when it closes.

Open **Logbook** at a terminal or the **ELD app** on the road to check your current duty, limits, daily totals, and reachable stops. If a roadside inspection catches you over hours, the officer orders a 10-hour hold and tells you what it does to your delivery deadline.

### Fatigue and nod-offs

Fatigue sits in four bands: **rested**, **worn** (40 percent), **drowsy** (60 percent), and **severe** (80 percent). A quiet yawn announces worn; a yawn plus "Getting drowsy." announces drowsy. From worn upward, **Tab** status screens name the band alongside the percentage ("Fatigue: 64 percent, drowsy."). To drop back out of a band, fatigue must fall 5 points below where that band started.

Night driving builds fatigue faster and trims your reaction time. Non-driving on-duty work (fueling, walk-arounds, docks, scales, inspections, waiting on repairs, chaining up, and sitting stopped at the wheel) builds fatigue at a third of the driving rate. Off-duty and sleeper time builds none.

When a **nod-off warning** sounds, answer it by steering or braking (left stick or left trigger on a controller). Miss it and the truck drifts onto the shoulder; three misses in a row force a stop. The first asleep departure counts as a preventable incident, and later ones become serious fatigued-driving violations.

Being tired slows down hands-on chores too: fueling, walk-arounds, chain work, and pulling into a stop take 10 percent longer when worn, 25 percent longer when drowsy, and 50 percent longer when severe (Relaxed pacing halves the extra time), and the menus reflect the longer minutes. Work done by someone else (docks, mechanics, officers) takes its usual time.

Inspectors and troopers notice severe fatigue. Any inspection, log check, or traffic stop while you are severe finishes with a **fatigue out-of-service order**: ten hours off duty right where you stand (with Relaxed pacing, your first one in a career is a warning instead).

If you are drowsy or severe on a load, **Call dispatch** offers **Too tired to drive safely** once per load. Dispatch pushes your appointment back by a 10-hour rest plus the drive to the nearest parking stop, and the load pays its base rate with no on-time bonus. Using that call a third time inside seven days costs dispatch trust, just like declining a load. On a team load with a rested co-driver, hand over the wheel instead.

### Recovering fatigue

- **30-minute break or a meal:** Takes off 35 points of fatigue for real.
- **Coffee or energy drinks:** Lend a temporary lift. Two hours after a coffee break it wears off ("The coffee has worn off." or the drink's own line) and the fatigue comes back; drinking a second one early adds no extra lift.
- **Proper sleep (sleeper parking, motel, or terminal bunk room):** Clears 8 points of fatigue per hour (80 points in 10 hours). Go to bed at 95 and you wake at 15; the sleep row tells you what you will wake at before you confirm. If a lot is full, it may offer a motel at your own expense.
- **Sleeping 10 hours in a full lot (poor rest):** Clears 5 points per hour and never drops fatigue below 30.
- **Emergency shoulder sleep:** Stop completely away from route points and press **T** (controller: **right bumper + D-pad down**) or use the pause menu (a nearby stop takes priority). It secures the truck and resets legal hours after 10 hours, but leaves your fatigue untouched, risks a ticket or damage, and does not pause your delivery deadline.

## Team driving

On runs too long for a single shift, a co-driver can take the wheel while you sleep in the bunk. Dispatch pairs company drivers with a classmate from Great Lakes Training Transport for the load; owner-operators stick with the co-driver they have teamed with most.

Whenever a load leaves the shipper needing a 10-hour break, your co-driver rides along for the entire trip even if you drive out first. Open the pause menu to see two rows naming your co-driver (for example, Dan):

- **Hand the wheel to Dan and ride along:** The drive continues at normal pace. You hear the truck and radio, while routine road announcements stay quiet.
- **Hand the wheel to Dan and sleep:** The co-driver's shift fast-forwards with the sound off, waking you when they need you along with a summary of miles driven and fuel stops.

The pickup menu offers the same pair once you are loaded: **Depart with Dan driving, and ride along** or **and sleep**. Press F1 on either row to hear the co-driver's name and pay split.

### Special load types on the board

- **Hot load:** Pays 25 percent more with a tight deadline.
- **Flexible delivery:** Pays 10 percent less with a generous deadline.
- **Team load:** Booked specifically for a team. Pays roughly a third more, with a deadline that leaves time for driver swaps rather than 10-hour parked breaks. You can still try one solo, but you will be late without a co-driver taking turns. Job trip hours and shipper route plans assume those swaps, and with planning hints on, the hint near the end of your hours names your co-driver instead of a sleep stop.

### While your co-driver is driving

Driving keys do nothing while you are out of the seat, though readout keys, **Tab**, driver apps, and the pause menu all stay active. If the radio is on, your co-driver might change the station or switch it off for a bit. Once you change it yourself, they leave it alone. A radio you turned off stays off.

The first time a load can be shared, the game tells you once at the shipper's dock or in the pause menu. After that, long loads note it on the dispatch board: "Co-driver: offered at the shipper's dock or from the pause menu, on your current hours."

Your co-driver drives with every assist, stops for fuel when low, pulls into open scales, and stops for troopers. They hand the wheel back:

- parked at the next stop with parking once your 10-hour rest is complete (which happens before a fresh co-driver runs out of hours);
- parked at a stop if their own hours expire first, telling you how much rest you got;
- at a rest area for their 30-minute break (or on the road if they could not reach one in time);
- at the receiver's gate so you can dock and deliver;
- whenever the truck takes damage or something needs your call.

Want the wheel back early? Pause and pick **Take the wheel back**. Your 10 hours must be unbroken, so driving early restarts your rest clock.

### Co-driver pay, hours, and history

- **Who they are:** Once you run a team load, **Career stats** adds a **Co-drivers** list showing everyone you have teamed with, loads run together, and a short note about each. **Driver status** reads the same bio for your current co-driver. Nothing reads it automatically unless you open it.
- **What it costs:** Company drivers earn half pay on miles the co-driver drives. Owner-operators pay the co-driver 30 percent of settlement for those miles while still covering fuel, tolls, and upkeep. Co-driver miles earn half career experience, settlement itemizes their miles and share, driving-style badges (such as a long no-toll run) do not unlock on team loads, and the road journal notes who drove.
- **Your hours:** Time while your co-driver drives counts as sleeper-berth time whether you ride along or sleep; 10 hours resets your legal clocks and clears 8 points of fatigue per hour, just like parked sleeper rest. Your co-driver keeps their own logbook. **Driver status**, **C**, and the **Take the wheel back** row tell you how much rest you have logged and when they hand back (for example, "Rest: 3.5 hours of 10 hours. Dan hands back in about 6.5 hours."), and the **ELD app** reads both logbooks. If you hand over while already rested, your co-driver drives until their own hours run out.

## Driver apps

Press **Tab** on the road (controller: **right bumper + Start**) and open **Driver apps**. You get seven cab apps: Radio, Navigation, Weather, Traffic, Truck stops, Road chatter, and ELD. Pick one with **Up/Down** and **Enter**; inside a report, **Up/Down** moves through lines, **Enter** repeats, and **Escape** steps back out (controller: **D-pad**, **A**, **B**).

### Radio app

The **Radio app** lets you turn the radio on or off, check whether streamer-safe mode is active, tune stations, manage favorites, browse or search the dial, and suggest a missing station.

- **Local stations:** Lists every Freight Fate station, your playlists and favorites, and terrestrial stations in range, with how far away each transmitter is.
- **Stations in range:** Adds web and satellite streams alongside local stations.
- **Search stations:** Type part of a name, call sign, or format and press **Enter**. Results come 25 at a time; use **Next page** / **Previous page** (or **Page Down** / **Page Up**) to see more.
- **Browse by category:** Lists all stations or a single category (such as Terrestrial or Web radio) in 25-row pages. In-range stations come first (local terrestrial ahead of web radio), duplicates appear once, and out-of-range stations follow. Stations marked out of range cannot be tuned from where you are.
- **Favorites:** Your shorter list of saved stations.

See [Radio and playlists](#radio-and-playlists) for the full guide to tuning, reception, and your own playlists.

### Navigation app

**Navigation** shows your next turn or exit, route progress, and next listed exit, useful whenever you want to double-check an instruction without waiting for the GPS. For the bigger picture of cities, highways, and upcoming stops, open **Map** under Driving status.

### Weather app

**Weather** tells you where the report comes from, how old it is, current conditions, and the suggested safe speed. With live weather enabled, it also lists any active weather alerts so you can replay a warning after it first sounded. Simulated weather includes a forecast of what is coming next (live reports may not have one). If a live update is still fetching or failed, the app tells you. Check the report's age before trusting it.

Pair the weather's safe speed with curves, grades, traffic, and tire wear. When you just want a quick check without opening a menu, press **V**.

### Traffic app

**Traffic** reports vehicles and slowdowns ahead, including distance and speed. If the road looks clear, it tells you there is no reported pinch in the next 20 miles. That is not a promise of an empty highway. Keep listening and check **L** before changing lanes.

### Truck stops app

**Truck stops** lists up to three upcoming stops within the next 100 miles along with their distance and services. Check it before committing: a public rest area often has parking without fuel or repairs. If nothing shows up within 100 miles, look further down the road in **Map** and weigh your fuel and hours before pressing on.

Once you pick a stop, press **T** while rolling to plan it and **X** to signal for its exit (controller: **right bumper + D-pad down**, then **D-pad down**). Once stopped there, **T** opens its services.

### Road chatter app

**Road chatter** collects informal CB reports from other drivers, including possible patrols ahead. Treat them as tips that can go stale: a patrol does not stay parked forever. In hours modes without enforcement, these reports stay quiet. Press **Alt+C** on the road to repeat the last CB report with an updated distance.

### ELD app

**ELD** gives your hours-of-service summary and the legal rest stops along your route so you can tell whether you can make the next stop before a clock runs out. It also reminds you of **Alt+A** (time used), **Alt+S** (break due), and **Alt+D** (shift limits). Recheck if traffic or weather slows your pace.

Press **Escape** (controller: **Start**) anytime to pause the drive; press **Start** again on a controller to resume. If you are stopped, **Call dispatch** lets you report a delay, ask about hours, road conditions, equipment, or your load, or request roadside help.

## Destination and settlement

Take the destination exit, follow the local streets into the receiver's yard, come to a complete stop at the gate, and press **Enter** (controller: **A**) to confirm arrival. Want to preview your numbers before unloading? Choose **Check paperwork** first. Choose **Dock and deliver** when you are ready to hand over the freight.

| Choice | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Dock and deliver | Unload the trailer, sign off on the paperwork, and open settlement. |
| Check paperwork | Preview facility, cargo, payout, deadline, damage, tolls, approved charges, driver charges, and net pay before finalizing. |
| Check arrival status | Review facility, cargo, current speed, and your next step. |

Unloading uses on-duty time. Once it wraps up, settlement walks through cargo condition, elapsed time, whether you beat the deadline, and the full money breakdown: gross pay, reimbursements, driver costs, net pay, cash balance, truck condition, and career progress.

Arriving on time earns a **10 percent bonus** (arriving even earlier does not add more, while late freight pays less). Company tolls and approved extra charges do not come out of your wages, and fines you already paid on the road are not deducted a second time.

Damaged cargo can trigger an exception deduction, a claim, or a rejected load with no delivery pay; fragile freight tolerates far less rough handling than bulk cargo. Hard braking, fast curves, and collisions all rattle what is in the trailer. Any charges your cash cannot cover become debt.

## Settings walkthrough

Every setting saves the moment you change it. Move with **Up** and **Down**, then press **Right** or **Enter** for the next value, or **Left** for the previous one (controller: **D-pad** and **A**). Press **F1** (controller: **Back**) on any setting in the game to hear its built-in explanation.

**Gameplay** holds Driving assistance, Difficulty and hours of service, World and traffic, and Controls. **Audio**, **Speech**, **Updates**, and **Problem reports** sit in their own sections.

### Driving assistance and speed keeper

Pick how much of the driving you want in your own hands:

- **Realistic:** Keeps core safety support while leaving steering and lane control to you (lane keeping off).
- **Balanced:** Adds partial lane keeping to help follow the road and helps stop at facilities.
- **All assists:** Full lane keeping centers the truck, takes route exits for you, and caps assisted descents at 55 miles per hour.

Tweaking any individual assist switches the preset label to **Custom** without touching your driving pace, hours mode, transmission, live data sources, or separate options. On the first-launch screen, pressing **Escape** keeps whatever you selected.

Individual assist options include **Downhill assistance** (Off, Light, Standard, Strong), **Following gap** (close, normal, far), emergency braking, lane-departure warnings, stop-and-go, exit speed, facility stopping, curves, and ramps.

- **Exit speed and facility stopping:** Exit speed assistance brakes once you enter the exit lane for your signaled exit (stopping or transition assistance can also provide that braking depending on your setup). Near a facility entrance, facility stopping manages throttle and brake and can pull away when a ramp light or stop sign clears. Touching the brake hands the pedals straight back to you, and you always pick optional stops and confirm delivery yourself.
- **Curve assistance:** Slows you before mapped bends and helps steer through them even when lane keeping is off (you still correct drift). Once clear, it lets you accelerate again. Braking overrides the slowing for that bend while steering help stays active. Cruise can pause for a tight bend and resume afterward. Assists generally use the engine brake for big speed drops or descents, switching to service brakes inside engine-brake noise zones.
- **Lane keeping:** **Full** keeps you centered and takes route exits while arrow taps (or left-stick pushes) request lane changes. **Partial** follows the road with gentle drift, leaving lane changes and exit entry to you. **Off** puts bends fully in your hands unless curve assistance is on. Letting go of the arrows or left stick squares the truck with the road (as does **Slash** while another key is held).
- **Speed keeper:** Handles low-speed stretches (local limits, corners, and queues) and hands off to adaptive cruise once you hit open road. With it off, work the pedals yourself until cruise can engage at 20 miles per hour.
- **Predictive cruise and curve callouts:** Predictive cruise reads roughly a mile and a half ahead for hills. Curve callouts can be turned off; **U** and **D** still answer either way.
- **Latching brake:** On by default for the keyboard brake (the controller left trigger does not latch). Tap Down, then press and hold for half a second until a click and spoken confirmation lock it on; tap Down or touch the throttle to release it. The throttle never latches, and changing presets leaves this setting and your cue volumes untouched.

### Difficulty and hours of service

Set your **driving pace** and your **hours-of-service enforcement** separately.

| Setting | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Driving mode | Choose Relaxed, Standard, or Real time. |
| Hours of service | Choose realistic or relaxed enforcement. Both keep the same 11-hour driving limit, 14-hour duty window, and 8-hour break rule; Relaxed lowers fines, inspections, and random hazards. |
| Hours of service planning hints | Off by default. When on, if your delivery is beyond your remaining legal hours, the cab suggests a break or sleep stop once with time to spare before the next hours warning (and names the last reachable stop if it recommends an earlier one). While rolling, picking a break or sleep from T plans that stop; press T again to cancel. Quiet and Urgent only speech skip the spoken hint. |

#### Choosing your driving pace

- **Relaxed:** Runs about 10 minutes of game time per real minute. Every driving system stays active, with fewer random hazards, more reaction time, calmer routine speech, and gentler fatigue and collision penalties.
- **Standard:** Runs twice as fast (about 20 minutes per real minute), so a full driving day takes half the real time, with decisions coming quicker and no extra forgiveness.
- **Real time:** Uses Standard's challenge level while matching your computer's clock and date on the road and while parked. Pair it with live weather if you want the highway to match the sky outside. Matching your clock never changes your delivery time remaining, career progress, or legal hours.

In Relaxed and Standard, time eases toward real time when you are crawling slowly, and waiting deliberately while parked runs at double your chosen pace. Taking breaks and sleep at stops advances time the same way in all three modes. You can switch driving modes from the pause menu mid-trip; the new pace kicks in the next time the truck stops.

## Audio, speech, and accessibility

Freight Fate speaks through your screen reader or system voice, including NVDA, JAWS, ZDSR, PC-Talker, BoYing, SenseReader, System Access, ZoomText, Narrator, VoiceOver, Orca, and Speech Dispatcher. You can even start a screen reader mid-drive and the game will pick it up without restarting.

If you want road calls on their own voice, assign a separate **Driving event voice** in Speech settings. Either **Control** key (or **Back** on a controller while speech is playing) cuts speech off immediately, and message review lets you step back through anything you missed.

Anytime a road sound catches you off guard, open **Learn game sounds**, select it, press **Enter** (or **A**) to hear it, and **F1** (or **Back**) for what it means. On a controller, **Haptics** adds vibration for road seams, lane edges, hazards, and braking.

### The game window

The game window renders both menus and the live drive for sighted and low-vision players, or anyone watching along:

- **Menus and screens:** Drawn as a large-print panel with the active row highlighted and a position count such as "3 of 9."
- **While driving:** Shows the road from behind your rig: your lane and neighboring lanes, traffic with brake lights and turn signals, hills and curves as they arrive, and road signs (speed limits, yellow curve advisories, work zones, exits, rest areas, weigh stations, state lines, and billboards). The sky matches local time of day, and rain, snow, fog, and storms appear live.
- **Dashboard:** Displays speed with the posted limit and cruise target marked, RPM and gear, fuel, air pressure, truck and cargo damage, warning lights (parking brake, low air, and more), turn signals, next bend and turn, time and weather, distance remaining, and a traffic view of every vehicle within half a mile.
- **Blind-spot lights:** Glow amber on the mirrors (and shade that lane on the traffic display) whenever a vehicle occupies the lane beside you. Wait for the light to go out before moving over, just like the "lane open" call and the **L** key.
- **Captions:** Display each spoken announcement on screen for a few seconds.

You can resize the window or run full screen and everything scales smoothly. Turn **Visual display** off under **Settings, Gameplay, Controls** to keep the window dark and save battery; the game also stops drawing automatically while minimized or (on macOS) hidden behind other windows.

## Radio and playlists

### Switching on and tuning

With the engine running, press **M** to turn the radio on or off. Tune forward with **Page Down** or **apostrophe**, and backward with **Page Up** or **semicolon**. Hold **Ctrl** with any tune key to jump between dial categories instead of stepping one station at a time.

Hold **Shift** with a tune key to adjust radio volume in 10 percent steps (**Page Up** or **Shift+semicolon** louder, **Page Down** or **Shift+apostrophe** quieter). Press **O** to save or remove the current station as a favorite, **Y** to hear full radio status, and **Shift+Y** to hear what song is playing.

On a controller, the **right stick** runs the radio: push left or right to tune, and up or down for volume (hold to keep stepping). Hold **right bumper** with the right stick up to toggle power, down to favorite, or left/right to switch categories. **Right bumper + left stick click** reads radio status, and **right bumper + Back** reads what is playing.

### Finding a station

Holding **Ctrl** while tuning jumps across categories: route music, Freight Fate stations, your playlists, favorites, terrestrial stations, AFN, satellite, international, fallback, web radio, and other stations. If you already know what you want or prefer searching by name, open the [Radio app](#radio-app).

Regional terrestrial stations fade as you drive out of transmitter range. **Favorites** never fade out. Once the local signal thins, the radio switches to the station's stream and reports its signal as streaming. If a non-favorite station drops out, the radio looks for a clear local station or a fallback; a stream that fails to connect leaves the dial for the rest of that session. **Roadhouse** and **Night Line** stay tuned everywhere on the map.

### Choosing music for your drive

- **Original (default):** Plays licensed menu and Roadhouse music.
- **Synthesized:** Uses music composed by the game along with the three classic tracks from Freight Fate 1.5, removing voiced breaks and fictional regional stations. Real radio streams and your own playlists still work unless streamer-safe mode hides them.

**Streamer-safe mode** restricts the dial to built-in safe stations. Paired with Synthesized music, only the synthesized Roadhouse remains; you can still toggle power, change volume, and check what is playing, while tuning keys do nothing.

### Playing your own music

Want your own playlist in the cab? Drop an **M3U**, **M3U8**, or **PLS** file into the **Playlists** folder inside your saves folder. Opening **Radio status** tells you the exact folder path and reloads any new playlists automatically.

Entries can point to local files, network paths, or stream URLs in **mp3, ogg, opus, flac, aac, wma**, or tracker formats (**.it, .xm, .s3m, .mod, .mo3**). Stations remember where you left off when you tune away. Your playlists play in order by default, or you can enable **Shuffle personal playlists** in Audio settings to hear every track once in a fresh random order each pass. Unreadable entries are skipped, slow streams get time to buffer, and if nothing can play, the game tells you why and names the folder.

Playlists can also include **YouTube links and livestreams**. Because Freight Fate does not bundle `yt-dlp`, install it yourself: drop `yt-dlp.exe` into your **Playlists** folder or put `yt-dlp` on your system PATH (on macOS, you can install it with Homebrew). Expect YouTube links to take a few extra seconds to start, and keep `yt-dlp` updated; an older copy can drop livestreams after about 30 seconds, and updating usually fixes streams that stop playing.

### Suggesting a station

Missing a favorite station? Open **Suggest a station** inside the Radio app (requires a connected account and Online services enabled). Pick its type, enter the name and stream URL (**Ctrl+V** pastes), and for AM/FM include the call sign and state (city and frequency are optional).

The service checks that the stream actually plays and is not already in the directory before queuing it for review. Approved stations appear on a later launch with no game update needed: matched transmitters play within reception range, and stations without a matched license location appear under web radio. You can also submit through the [station suggestion page](https://orinks.net/freight-fate/suggest-a-station), and [streamurl.link](https://streamurl.link) can help you track down stream URLs.

## Achievements and career progress

Open **Achievements** from the main menu and pick a saved career, or open it directly from the terminal or pause menu to see your current driver's progress. The screen announces how many achievements that driver has unlocked, organized by category. Move with **Up/Down**, press **Enter** to repeat, and **Escape** to step back (controller: **D-pad**, **A**, **B**).

The top row, **Sort**, changes how categories and entries are ordered: earned first (default), newest first, locked first, A to Z, or original order, using **Left/Right** or **Enter**, and remembers your preference. Each category also has its own Sort row that applies to that category until you leave. **Account achievements** under Online sorts the same way.

Earned achievements include full descriptions; locked ones hold back some detail, and hidden achievements keep their names under wraps until you unlock them. You can browse achievements anytime without loading or changing a career.

To plan what comes next in your career, use three screens at the terminal together: **Career plan** for your next milestone after your first dispatch, **Career stats** for your level, reputation, deliveries, qualifications, and record, and **Business status** for your equipment and path toward ownership or own authority.

## Online sharing and cloud backups

Everything online lives in the [Online menu](#online-menu). You can look up other drivers right away, and connect an orinks.net account whenever you want cloud backups or a public profile.

To connect, choose **Set up orinks.net account**. The game opens the sign-in page in your browser with an activation code and links automatically once you sign in (the screen can also spell or copy the code if needed). Connecting turns on both profile sharing and cloud backups by default, and you can switch either one off right afterward.

- **Public profile:** Can display lifetime earnings, activity, achievements, and road journal, but never shares your current cash, full saves, coordinates, or exact location. Detailed career stats require a validated private backup. Turning off sharing hides your profile without stopping backups. From the setup page on orinks.net, you can also change driver names and sign out other computers.
- **Cloud backups:** Upload privately after every save, keeping up to 10 accepted revisions per career across up to 10 careers. Restoring checks the signature and keeps the replaced local save as a fallback. **Back up this career now** uploads immediately, and removing an old cloud career never touches your local save.

## Common questions

### Do I have to buy a truck?

Not at all. Company drivers drive carrier trucks with fuel, repairs, and insurance covered. Buying your own truck opens with the owner-operator buy-in at level 18. Check **Business status** in the game or [Business status](#business-status) here for the full list of requirements and costs.

### What are the fleets on Career stats?

As a company driver, dispatch moves you into better carrier fleets as you level up, at no cost: yard standard at level 1, regional at level 4, long-haul at level 9, premium at level 13, and first pick of the yard at level 17. **Career stats** tells you your current fleet and when the next one opens.

### Why am I in a different truck on some loads?

Before level 9, dispatch assigns whichever spare fits the load (a sleeper for a long haul, a heavy-spec tractor for heavy freight, or a day cab for a short hop), and each spare keeps its own fuel and wear. Once you reach level 9, you keep one tractor for every load until you step up to the next fleet at level 13 and level 17.

### Why am I not in the truck my level earns?

Low dispatch trust, a suspended CDL, debt, or your driving record can put a hold on higher-tier equipment. **Career stats** tells you why and what brings your truck back.

### What is the difference between reputation and dispatch trust?

**Reputation** is a 0-to-100 score built from clean deliveries and dented by violations, grouped into tiers: Outstanding (90+), Well regarded (75 to 89), Established (60 to 74), Unproven (40 to 59; every new driver starts at 50), Shaky, Damaged, and Ruined. Dispatch trust lines include it, such as "Dispatch trust: full. Reputation 85 out of 100." Under **Settings, Speech, Score display**, you can hear both description and number, percentage only, or description only.

**Dispatch trust** is what the carrier lets you do right now: how much of the board you see, how many refusals you get, and which truck you drive. It weighs your CDL, driving record, and debt alongside reputation. See [Dispatch trust](#dispatch-trust).

### How many levels are there?

Thirty. Owner-operator buy-in and own authority unlock along the way; see [Business status](#business-status).

### Why can I not accept a load?

It likely requires a certificate, endorsement, or trailer program you do not have yet. Press **F1** on the load to hear what is missing, then check [Licenses and training](#licenses-and-training).

### Why did I get a citation at a weigh station?

When you hear "Open weigh station" (or the weigh-station cue with Quiet or Urgent only speech), every truck must pull in. Signal with **X**, stop at the scale, and press **T** to check in. With full lane keeping, the truck signals for the scale on its own, and with facility stopping assistance on it stops there for you too. Closed scales are never announced; just roll right past them.

### Why is the speed limit 55 on a fast highway?

Some states, California among them, set a lower speed limit for trucks than for cars. Pressing **S** names the state whenever you are under a truck speed limit.

### Why do I keep getting speeding tickets?

Press **S** to check the posted limit and how far over you are. The dash overspeed chime is a free warning; slow down when you hear it. Automatic speed control stays at most 5 mph over the limit and prepares ahead for sharp limit drops.

### I missed my exit. What now?

Keep driving and follow the turnaround guidance to the next safe turnaround. Press **R** to check how far you have left.

### The truck will not move. What should I check?

Walk through the four basics: fuel, engine running, air pressure at 100 psi, and parking brake released. Severe damage, 100 percent worn components, or a suspended CDL can also keep you parked. See [When you need help](#when-you-need-help).

### How do I reverse?

In an automatic: come to a complete stop, release the brake, then press and hold the brake again. While backing up, the throttle stops the truck. To go forward again, stop with the throttle, release it, then press and hold the throttle again. See [Driving controls](#driving-controls).

### How do I know when a hill ends?

Press **G** to hear the grade under your wheels, how far it runs, and the next grade worth planning for.

### How do I change stations or play my own music?

With the engine running, press **M** to turn the radio on and **Page Up** or **Page Down** to tune. Put M3U, M3U8, or PLS playlists in the **Playlists** folder inside your saves folder (**Radio status** tells you the exact path). See [Radio and playlists](#radio-and-playlists).

### Can I hear a weather warning again?

Yes. With live weather on, the **Weather app** under Driver apps lists active alerts, and **Comma** steps back through recent spoken messages.

### Can I steer the truck myself?

Yes. Pick the **Realistic** preset (or set lane keeping to **Off**) under **Settings, Gameplay, Driving assistance**. See [Taking curves](#taking-curves) for how steering by sound works.

### Can I play in braille only?

Yes. Set **Output** to **Braille only** under **Settings, Speech**. This requires NVDA or JAWS.

## When you need help

If the truck will not roll, check the basics first: fuel, engine, air pressure, and parking brake. Heavy damage, worn-out parts, or a suspended CDL can also stop you; roadside help can restore fuel or patch the truck enough to move.

If your controller is not responding, walk through [If the controller doesn't respond](#if-the-controller-doesnt-respond).

Trying to rest? **T** plans a stop while rolling and opens a supported stop's services once you are stopped. For emergency shoulder sleep, come to a complete stop first. If you missed a destination exit, follow the turnaround guidance.

Still stuck, or ran into a bug? Open **Problem reports** in Settings or **Report a problem** on the main menu. **Where the game log is saved** shows where your session transcript and log live. They stay on your computer unless you choose to send a report.
