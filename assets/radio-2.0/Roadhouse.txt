Realism changes
Each change is flagged here. Nothing else in the spoken copy was rewritten.

1. Prairie Link draft: dropped "out and back" before "on runs that keep you
in the region." home_time_policy is inert, and "out and back" promises a
home-time pattern a dispatcher cannot honor.

2. Great Lakes draft: dropped "more stops." short_haul_bias weights shorter
miles on the national board. It does not add more pickup or delivery stops.
Stop pay is higher than the other nationals, but the board does not dispatch
more stops.

3. Winterset draft: "beef and produce through Garden City and McAllen"
became "packed meat out of Garden City, produce through McAllen." Garden
City has Tyson Fresh Meats, a food processor that ships refrigerated packed
meat, not live cattle. Winterset's bonuses are food and refrigerated only;
livestock is a different cargo class. McAllen has cold storage and a food
processor, not a packing plant, so "beef through McAllen" invented freight.

4. Open Hearth draft: "flatbed and step-deck drivers" became "flatbed and
heavy-haul drivers." The game has no step-deck equipment. Machinery loads
require the heavy-haul credential, the same as Great Shield.

5. Great Shield draft: "yards in the oil patch" became "yards in Alberta."
Grande Prairie and Red Deer sit in oil country, but the facilities there are
lumber (Canfor Grande Prairie) and food and retail, not oilfield freight.

6. Lone Mesa draft: "down to the border crossings" became "down through the
Valley." The only Mexico crossing in the game is Laredo. Lone Mesa has no
Laredo terminal and no cross_border flag. McAllen is a Valley terminal, not
a border-crossing yard.

7. Androscoggin draft: tag line "Northeast freight, closer to home" became
"Northeast freight, regional miles." "Closer to home" is a home-time
promise. home_time_policy is inert. This matches the other regional tags.

8. Olentangy draft: dropped "and the ports" after "through the plants."
Columbus, Cincinnati, Louisville, Indianapolis, and Detroit have automotive
or manufacturing plants, but none of those terminals has a port or port
terminal in the facility data. Cross-border work is real and stays.

Host lines, the legal ID, the liners, and the other seven carrier drafts
were left as written.

Music beds
Today's hiring spots are dry voice-over with a light broadcast compress.
There is no music under them. Legal IDs already get short imaging SFX
(whoosh and riser) from assets in sounds.pak. Songs and station jingles live
in music.pak.

For these carrier spots, cut a short instrumental bed per ad, or a small
shared pool, on Eleven Music eleven_music_v2_5, about 20 to 30 seconds,
instrumental only, so the voice sits on top. No lyrics. Leave a clean intro
and a clean out.

Suggested colors, not final cues:

- Roadhouse and general national hiring: warm Americana, acoustic and a little harmonica, mid-tempo, radio-clean

- Prairie Link: pedal steel and a soft shuffle

- Winterset: cooler, tighter, a little night-highway

- Double Springs: a brighter, rolling country-rock bed

- Open Hearth and Great Shield: more guitar and weight, still instrumental

- Longspur and the Canadian board: a slightly wider, open-prairie feel

- Chehalis and Chatanika: a wetter, more northern color

- Tehachapi and Lone Mesa: drier, a little more Southwest

- Androscoggin and Olentangy: a plainer, regional FM bed

Beds are mixed under the VO, not used as standalone songs. After Joshua
locks copy, generate beds and voice separately, then mix. The Roadhouse
would start carrying these carrier spots; other format stations can keep
the existing rotation plus the new hiring ads.
